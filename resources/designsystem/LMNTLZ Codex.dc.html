SETTLED
Six champions. Two front, three middle, one back. The row sizes never change — what you choose is who sits where.
SETTLED
You keep two squads: an attack six you command yourself, and a defense six the engine runs when you are attacked.
Distance from row R to row T is the number of occupied rows you cross to get there — counting the target's row and not your own. A champion may target row T if that distance is at most its reach.

Your own rows count against you. At full formation only your two front-row champions, plus any reach-2 champion in the middle, can strike an enemy at all.
Empty rows are free. A row with nobody left alive stops counting, so range opens as the battle wears on. A squad gains reach as it loses champions.
THE BACK SEAT
Row 1 cannot be targeted by anything while your middle and front hold — and cannot attack either. A reach-2 champion there can still heal, buff and cleanse the whole squad. A reach-1 champion there does almost nothing. The trap is deliberate and knowable.
One rule, no exceptions. Reach governs allies as well as enemies — a heal is range-limited exactly as an attack is. Champions never move; only the count of living rows changes.

Combat is turn-based and server-resolved. You command your six on offense; the engine runs the defense. Every committed action is in flight for a moment while the Court decides it — a miss ends resolution there, with no chip damage and no status.
Learning a champion's cooldowns is learning their song. No two powers breathe at the same rhythm; a squad of fast rechargers opens hard and fades, a slow one answers after turn four.

THE VISIBLE WALL
Before you commit, one of a warden's defensive squads is surfaced in full — six champions in their 2/3/1 formation. Scout it, then bring the six whose Forces open its doors.
THE HIDDEN SIX
Their second wall stays sealed. On an ambush it answers instead of the one you scouted — and everything you prepared is aimed at the wrong doors.
AMBUSH CHANCE
2% per consecutive attacking win. The longer your offensive streak runs, the more often the Hidden Six answers the door. A loss resets it to zero. Win enough and the Court starts to expect you.
Both walls keep their own streak. A defensive squad that keeps turning attackers away builds a record of its own — visible to anyone scouting it.

Passes & Pricing

Last updated 29 July 2026

LMNTLZ is a competitive fantasy squad battler. All twenty-seven champions are unlocked from the moment you sign in, identically, for everybody — there is no roster to collect and nothing to pull for. Skill is reading your opponent's weaknesses and building against them.

What is sold

One product: the boost pass. It doubles the shards you earn, within a daily cap:

  • Attack boost — double shards from your first ten attack victories each day.
  • Defense boost — double shards from your first ten defense holds each day.

A pass grants both, every day it is active. It is bought in seven durations, and every price is a multiple of $5.

Four promises about the storefront

Nothing auto-renews

There is no subscription. You buy a pass; when it ends, it ends. Nothing is charged again, there is nothing to cancel, and there is no cancellation flow to find your way out of. If you want more time you buy more time, deliberately.

Passes add, they never replace

Buying while a pass is still running extends it. Nothing is reset and nothing is forfeited, so there is no penalty for topping up early and no reason to wait for a lapse.

Shards cannot be bought

There is no second currency and no way to buy progress directly. A pass multiplies what you earn by playing, up to a daily cap. If you do not play, it does nothing.

The ceiling is public, and it is $160 a year

Because the boost is capped daily and the year pass is the best rate, the most anyone can spend to gain an advantage is $160 in a year — nobody rational buys thirteen 4-week passes at $260 for the same 364 days. A fully equipped paying player and a fully equipped free player end up in exactly the same place; the paying player got there sooner.

The longest pass grants the cap and never more. It is a discount and a convenience, not a higher tier. There is no product that raises the ceiling, and there never will be one — that is the whole point of publishing it.

Buying

Passes are bought from inside the game, after you sign in, so the pass can be attached to your account. An entitlement belongs to your account, not to the store that sold it — if you later sign in through a different platform, everything you paid for is still there.
